INTRODUCTION
================================================================================
Introduction

The state of Florida created a medical cannabis program in 2016, with implementation in 2017 [ 1 , 2 ]. As of 2025, there are over 900,000 registrants in Florida’s medical cannabis program [ 3 ], positioning it as the largest by total participant enrollment when compared with other state medical cannabis programs in the USA [ 4 ]. As medical cannabis programs continue to expand throughout the USA and globally, notable gaps remain in the evidence regarding medical cannabis safety and effectiveness when used as a therapeutic or as a treatment adjuvant for several types of health conditions [ 5 ].

The state legislature in Florida established the Consortium for Medical Marijuana Clinical Outcomes Research, hereafter referred to as the “Consortium,” to conduct, disseminate, and support scientific research on clinical outcomes of cannabis use [ 1 ]. Since the inception of the Consortium in 2019, it has expanded its membership to include eleven public and private universities. Investigators within member institutions have access to Consortium-developed research infrastructure, including a grants program, the Medical Marijuana Outcomes Research Repository (MEMORY) and data from the Medical Marijuana and Me (M3) prospective survey, regulatory consultation services to support clinical research, and a community outreach program. The Consortium supports the Cannabis Clinical Outcomes Research Conference (CCORC) as a key component of community outreach and dissemination of research findings across clinical and scientific communities.

CONCLUSION
================================================================================
Conclusion

The fifth annual CCORC presented its most ambitious scientific program to date. Preliminary findings from several studies of cannabis safety, use patterns, and effectiveness that had public health and safety implications were presented by attendees. Keynote addresses from established investigators in cannabis research emphasized the current state of evidence for the emerging roles of cannabis as a therapeutic in pain management, neurological disorders, and psychiatric care, while others called for nuanced consideration of cannabis risk-benefit in distinct clinical subgroups, in addition to a comprehensive state of the science for novel cannabinoid delivery systems (particularly in cancer treatments and for symptom relief). Attendees also received information about resources regarding research infrastructure, including the process for acquiring de-identified medical cannabis program dispensing data within MEMORY. The sixth annual CCORC will be held in November of 2026 in Lake Nona, Orlando, FL, in the USA. The conference website will post updates regarding program developments, meeting details, a call for abstracts, and a call for symposia and workshop proposals.
